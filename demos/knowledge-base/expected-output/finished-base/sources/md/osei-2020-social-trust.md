# Social Trust and Turnout: A Cross-National Description

# Daniel Osei

# *Talenian Journal of Comparative Politics* 7(1): 12–39, 2020

## Abstract

I describe the cross-national relationship between aggregate social trust and turnout in 38 democracies, using survey waves from 2005 to 2018. The association is positive and moderately strong. The paper is descriptive and makes no causal claim.

## 1. Purpose

This is a description, and it is meant to be read as one. Trust and turnout travel together across countries. Whether one produces the other is a question this design cannot address, and the paper does not pretend otherwise.

## 2. Data

Trust is the country-year mean of the standard interpersonal-trust item. Turnout is voting-age-population turnout in the national election nearest each survey wave. Coverage is 38 democracies and 214 country-years.

## 3. Results

The bivariate correlation is 0.52. A ten-point difference in the share expressing trust corresponds to about four points of turnout. The relationship holds within regions and is not driven by any single country.

> We document an association, not a mechanism. Nothing here licenses the inference that raising trust would raise turnout.

## 4. What this cannot tell you

Reverse causation is live: participating in elections may build trust rather than follow from it. Confounding is worse.

> The cross-section cannot separate trust from the many things that travel with it, from institutional quality to compulsory-voting rules.

Readers wanting a causal account should look to designs with variation that is plausibly unrelated to the outcome. This paper supplies the pattern such a design would have to explain.
