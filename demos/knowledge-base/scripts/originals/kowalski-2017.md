## Abstract

Using complete friendship rosters from 24 Veremian secondary schools, I show that
first-time turnout clusters within peer networks beyond what shared neighbourhood and
household characteristics predict.

## 1. Introduction

Turnout runs in families. This paper asks whether it also runs in friendships, and
whether the clustering survives adjustment for the things friends have in common before
they become friends.

## 2. Design

Rosters were collected in the final school year and linked to validated turnout at the
first election each student was eligible to vote in. I compare observed clustering with
clustering in networks rewired at random within school.

## 3. Results

Observed clustering exceeds the rewired benchmark in 21 of 24 schools. The excess is
concentrated in reciprocated friendships.

## 4. Discussion

The pattern is consistent with peer influence and equally consistent with selection on
unobserved political interest. The design cannot separate them, and the paper should not
be cited as evidence that peer effects cause turnout.
