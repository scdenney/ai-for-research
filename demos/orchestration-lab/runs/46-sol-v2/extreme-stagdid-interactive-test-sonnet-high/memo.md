# Adjudication

The five estimators agree on the substantive result. Naive county- and year-fixed-effects TWFE estimates an ATT of −0.0365 (clustered SE 0.0133). Callaway–Sant’Anna gives −0.0398 (SE 0.0124) with not-yet-treated controls and −0.0400 (SE 0.0119) with never-treated controls. Sun–Abraham also gives −0.0400 (SE 0.0118), while Roth–Sant’Anna’s staggered estimator is somewhat larger at −0.0471 (SE 0.0116). Moving from TWFE to heterogeneity-robust estimators therefore changes the estimate by roughly 0.3–1.1 log points, not its sign or practical interpretation.

The Goodman–Bacon decomposition shows why the classic warning has limited empirical bite here. Clean treated-versus-never-treated comparisons account for 86.28% of TWFE’s identifying weight. Earlier-versus-later-treated comparisons, made while the later cohort is still untreated, contribute another 8.33%. Only 5.39% comes from the potentially contaminated later-versus-earlier-treated comparisons, where an already-treated cohort serves as the control. The decomposition’s 2×2 weights are positive; the danger is that this comparison type can imply negative weights on heterogeneous group-time effects. Here its weighted contribution is +0.00025, slightly offsetting rather than producing the negative TWFE estimate.

Pre-treatment evidence is broadly reassuring but not definitive. Relative to event time −1, the estimates at −3 and −2 are 0.025 and 0.024, and both pointwise 95% intervals include zero. The joint pre-trend test has *p* = 0.168. Thus, this short panel does not detect a parallel-trends violation, although the confidence intervals still admit modest departures. Post-treatment effects become increasingly negative through event time 2, so treatment-effect dynamics are present even though they do not materially destabilize the pooled conclusion.

I would report the never-treated Callaway–Sant’Anna ATT: approximately −0.040 (SE 0.012), or about a 4% decline in teen employment. It uses a fixed, sizable comparison population and agrees almost exactly with Sun–Abraham and the not-yet-treated specification. That conclusion depends on respecting package-specific cohort coding: `did` correctly retains `first.treat = 0`, whereas `staggered` requires recoding never-treated counties to `Inf`; blindly passing the zero sentinel to `staggered` silently changes the estimand. The staggered-adoption critique therefore changes which estimate I privilege and what diagnostics I require, but not the substantive conclusion for this dataset. This is evidence that TWFE happens to be close here, not that plain TWFE is generally safe under staggered adoption.
