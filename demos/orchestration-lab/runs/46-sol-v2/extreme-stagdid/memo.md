# Adjudication

The staggered-adoption critique is real in principle, but it does not change the substantive conclusion in this panel. The naive two-way fixed-effects (TWFE) estimate is −0.0365 log points (SE 0.0133). Callaway–Sant’Anna gives −0.0398 (SE 0.0121) with never- and not-yet-treated counties as controls and −0.0400 (SE 0.0120) with never-treated counties only. Sun–Abraham is also −0.0400 (SE 0.0118), while the Roth–Sant’Anna efficient estimate is somewhat larger at −0.0471 (SE 0.0116). Thus every estimator indicates a modest negative effect, and changing the estimator moves the point estimate by only about 0.003–0.011 log points relative to TWFE.

The Bacon decomposition explains that agreement. Clean treated-versus-never-treated comparisons receive 86.28% of TWFE’s identifying weight and contribute −0.03515 to the −0.03655 coefficient. Earlier-versus-later comparisons, which use not-yet-treated counties as controls, receive another 8.33%. Only 5.39% lies in later-versus-earlier comparisons, where an already-treated cohort serves as the control; that potentially contaminated component contributes just +0.00025. The Bacon shares themselves are nonnegative. The negative-weight concern applies to implicit weights on underlying group-time effects within the contaminated comparison, not to a negative reported Bacon share. Here that channel is small and slightly attenuates, rather than creates, the negative TWFE estimate.

The event study is compatible with parallel pre-trends, although it cannot prove them. Relative to the normalized period at event time −1, the two plotted leads are +0.0250 (SE 0.0181) at −3 and +0.0245 (SE 0.0142) at −2; both pointwise 95% intervals include zero. The omnibus pre-treatment Wald test also fails to reject (p = 0.168). The short pre-period and finite power counsel against treating this as affirmative proof. Post-treatment estimates become negative, reaching −0.051 at event time 1 and about −0.10 to −0.14 at times 2–3; later event times represent fewer cohorts, so the apparent exposure pattern should not be overinterpreted.

I would report the never-treated Callaway–Sant’Anna simple ATT: approximately −0.040 log points (SE 0.012), or roughly a 4% decline in teen employment. The near-identical not-yet-treated and Sun–Abraham results are useful robustness checks. This supports using a modern estimator for transparent identification while showing that, for this dataset—not staggered designs generally—the classic TWFE problem is quantitatively minor.
