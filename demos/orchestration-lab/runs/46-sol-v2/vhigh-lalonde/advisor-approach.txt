Do not proceed unchanged. The specification curve is sensible, but trimming creates a material estimand defect.

Untrimmed estimates target the ATT for all 185 NSW treated units. Trimming away treated units outside CPS support instead targets an overlap-subpopulation ATT. Its gap from the full-sample experimental benchmark cannot be interpreted as matching bias or “recovery”; treatment effects may differ in the retained subgroup. Separate trimmed results as a different-estimand sensitivity analysis, or construct specification-specific experimental overlap benchmarks by applying the same pre-treatment score rule to both NSW arms. Do not treat every cell as estimating one common effect.

Also correct these details:

- If the score is refit after trimming, recheck overlap using the refitted score. The initial intersection no longer guarantees final-score support.
- Form ATT subclasses from treated-score quintiles, not pooled-sample quintiles—the 15,992 CPS controls would dominate pooled cutpoints. Require treated and controls in every stratum, merge empty/sparse adjacent strata, and report balance and counts.
- CR1 clustering by reused control is only a conditional-on-the-realized-match sandwich calculation. It captures control reuse but not propensity-score estimation, support selection, or nearest-neighbor matching discrepancy; a \(t\) critical value over unique controls does not repair that. Use an Abadie–Imbens-style analytic variance appropriate to estimated-score matching, or explicitly label the CR1 intervals as conditional/descriptive rather than full 95% sampling intervals.
- The stratification plug-in variance likewise conditions on estimated scores and cutpoints. Prefer a full bootstrap that refits the score and reconstructs strata for subclassification only, or an appropriate stacked sandwich estimator.
- Do not infer “recovery” from overlapping intervals. Ideally report uncertainty for each gap from the randomized benchmark, accounting for the shared NSW treated outcomes.

Proceed after those corrections. Otherwise favorable trimmed estimates could appear to recover the benchmark simply because the treated population changed, while the intervals overstate their inferential defensibility.
