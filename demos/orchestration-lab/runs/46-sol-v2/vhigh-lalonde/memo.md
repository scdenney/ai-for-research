# Adjudication

The experimental benchmark is **$1,794**: NSW participants assigned to treatment earned that much more in 1978, on average, than the randomized NSW controls (95% CI $474 to $3,115). Replacing those controls with the CPS sample immediately breaks the comparison. The naive NSW-treated minus CPS-control contrast is **−$8,498**, a gap of −$10,292 from the benchmark.

Propensity-score adjustment does not recover the benchmark robustly. With demographics alone, 1-nearest-neighbor (1-NN) matching estimates **−$2,798** both before and after common-support trimming. Five-stratum estimates are still farther away: **−$4,137** without trimming and **−$4,003** with trimming. All four intervals lie below the experimental point estimate. These failures are not simply a lack-of-overlap result: trimming removes CPS controls but retains all 185 treated NSW units, and it barely changes the demographic specifications.

Recovery appears only under favorable choices. Adding 1974 and 1975 earnings to the score moves 1-NN estimates to **$1,712** without trimming and **$1,759** with trimming—gaps of only −$82 and −$36. Yet changing only the estimator to five score strata yields **−$144** without trimming and **$1,290** with trimming. Thus, pre-program earnings are consequential, trimming helps the coarse stratification estimate, and 1-NN is the detail that most nearly reproduces the benchmark. The pattern is specification dependence, not invariance.

Conditioning is doing something real but limited. It reweights or selects CPS controls to resemble the NSW treated group on the variables entered into the logit score. It cannot balance omitted determinants, make the CPS sample randomized, prove conditional ignorability, or repair a misspecified score. The diagnostics reinforce that distinction: demographic 1-NN achieves a maximum absolute standardized mean difference of 0.075 on its included covariates and nevertheless misses badly, while the benchmark-recovering earnings 1-NN rows retain a maximum imbalance near 0.40. Numerical agreement is therefore not evidence that the observational design has recreated random assignment. The HC3 intervals are defensible post-design uncertainty summaries, but they condition on the estimated score and support rule; interval overlap should not be read as an equivalence test.

The gap column is likewise descriptive, not a direct estimate of matching bias. The randomized benchmark has sampling uncertainty, and the benchmark and observational estimates share the same NSW treated outcomes. A formal test of their difference would need to account for that dependence. Here the specification curve is primarily a design-sensitivity audit.

A paper may legitimately claim that, in this LaLonde/CPS comparison, propensity-score matching **can** reproduce the experimental point estimate when lagged earnings are included and 1-NN replacement matching is used. It may also claim that equally transparent alternatives fail, supporting the Smith–Todd fragility critique. It may not claim, without qualification, that “matching works,” that “matching fails,” or that a recovered benchmark validates the identifying assumptions. The defensible verdict is **recovery only under favorable specifications**.
