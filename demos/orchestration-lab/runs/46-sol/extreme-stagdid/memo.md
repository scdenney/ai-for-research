# Adjudication

The estimators tell a notably consistent substantive story. The naive county- and year-fixed-effects estimate is −0.0365 (clustered SE 0.0133). Callaway–Sant’Anna gives −0.0398 (SE 0.0123) with not-yet-treated controls and −0.0400 (SE 0.0119) with never-treated controls. Sun–Abraham is also −0.0400 (SE 0.0118), while the Roth–Sant’Anna efficient staggered estimator is somewhat larger at −0.0471 (SE 0.0116). Thus, changing the estimator moves the point estimate within a narrow range of roughly 3.7–4.7 log points; it does not reverse the sign or alter the conclusion that treatment reduced teen employment.

The Goodman–Bacon decomposition explains why plain TWFE is not badly misleading here. Clean treated-versus-never-treated comparisons receive 86.28% of the identifying weight. Another 8.33% compares earlier-treated counties with later-treated counties while the latter are still untreated. Only 5.39% rests on the potentially contaminated “later versus earlier treated” comparisons in which already-treated units serve as controls. The Bacon weights themselves are positive; the concern is that this comparison type can induce negative implicit weights on heterogeneous group-time effects. In these data its weighted contribution is slightly positive (0.00025), so it modestly offsets rather than creates the negative TWFE estimate. This is a small exposure to the classic failure mode, not evidence that the failure mode is impossible.

The event study is also broadly reassuring, with qualifications. Relative to event time −1, estimates at −3 and −2 are 0.025 and 0.024, and both 95% intervals include zero. The joint pre-trend test has *p* = 0.168. We therefore do not detect a violation of parallel trends. That is supporting evidence, not proof: the panel supplies only a short pre-treatment window, and the intervals admit modest departures. Post-treatment effects become more negative with exposure, reaching about −0.14 at event time 2, so effect dynamics are empirically relevant.

I would report the Callaway–Sant’Anna estimate using never-treated controls: ATT = −0.040 (SE 0.012), or about a 4% decline in teen employment. The never-treated group is large, fixes the comparison population, and produces virtually the same answer as not-yet-treated controls and Sun–Abraham. The staggered-adoption critique therefore changes which estimate I privilege and how I interpret it, but not the substantive conclusion for this dataset.
