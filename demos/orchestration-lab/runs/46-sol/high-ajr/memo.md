# Memo: How much weight does the AJR replication bear?

The headline result replicates in the 64-country base sample. OLS associates a one-unit increase in protection against expropriation risk with 0.522 log points higher 1995 income. Instrumenting institutions with log settler mortality raises the coefficient to 0.944. The first stage has the expected negative sign: a one-unit increase in log settler mortality predicts a 0.607-point reduction in the institutions index. Its excluded-instrument partial F is 22.95, comfortably above the conventional rule-of-thumb threshold of 10. Thus, within the bivariate specification, the data reproduce both the direction and the characteristic OLS–2SLS gap in the AJR headline.

The result also survives the two full-sample control perturbations, although the first-stage margin narrows. Adding absolute latitude gives OLS and 2SLS coefficients of 0.468 and 0.996, with a first-stage F of 13.09. Adding the Africa and Asia indicators gives coefficients of 0.434 and 0.839, with F = 11.01. These estimates preserve the positive IV effect and its larger magnitude relative to OLS. They are not weak by the stipulated F < 10 rule, but the continent-control specification is close enough to the cutoff that “strong under all controls” would be too categorical.

The sample perturbations do not furnish reliable effect estimates. Excluding Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and the United States lowers the first-stage F to 8.65. The resulting 2SLS coefficient, 1.281, is therefore weakly identified and should not be read as evidence that the effect grows outside the neo-Europes. In the Africa-only sample, the first stage nearly disappears (coefficient −0.108; F = 0.30), while the 2SLS coefficient inflates to 2.400. That point estimate is not informative about an Africa-specific causal effect. Crucially, these weak-instrument results neither confirm the headline nor overturn it: they show that these restricted samples do not identify the parameter with useful precision through this instrument.

The manuscript may claim that it reproduces the AJR bivariate 2SLS result and that the positive, larger-than-OLS estimate persists after adding latitude or broad continent controls. It may also report that identification is sensitive to sample composition. It may not claim that the causal effect is established generally, robust to removing the neo-Europes, or demonstrated within Africa. First-stage strength addresses instrument relevance only; it does not validate the exclusion restriction or rule out other threats to a causal interpretation.
