# Memo: AJR IV replication and stress tests

The AJR headline replicates in the 64-country base sample. On the same complete-case sample, the OLS association between protection against expropriation risk and log income is 0.522, whereas the 2SLS estimate using log settler mortality as the excluded instrument is 0.944. The first stage has the expected negative sign: a one-unit increase in log settler mortality is associated with a 0.607 decrease in the institutions measure. Its partial first-stage F statistic is 22.95, which clears the conventional F ≥ 10 screening rule. Thus, the core calculation is reproducible and is not weakly identified by that rule of thumb.

The two observed-control specifications also retain positive IV estimates. Adding absolute latitude yields an IV coefficient of 0.996 and a first-stage coefficient of −0.510 (F = 13.09). Adding Africa and Asia indicators yields an IV coefficient of 0.839 and a first-stage coefficient of −0.533 (F = 11.01). These results are consistent with the headline in sign and broad magnitude. Their first stages nevertheless deserve qualified language: both pass F ≥ 10, but are borderline, particularly the continent-controls specification. They support robustness to these two specific observed-control adjustments, not a general conclusion that the estimate is insensitive to arbitrary controls.

The sample-restriction exercises do not provide interpretable confirmation. Excluding Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and the United States produces an IV estimate of 1.281, but the first-stage F falls to 8.65. Because this is below the conventional threshold, the point estimate is unreliable as a robustness test; it neither confirms nor overturns the full-sample result. Within Africa, the first stage nearly disappears (−0.108; F = 0.30), despite a reported 2SLS coefficient of 2.400. That coefficient should not be treated as evidence about an Africa-specific effect because the instrument supplies essentially no identifying variation in this subsample.

Accordingly, the manuscript may claim that the full-sample headline replicates and that the positive IV estimate persists after the latitude and continent-control specifications. It may not claim that the causal effect is established across sample definitions, that it applies within Africa, or that it survives excluding neo-Europes. Finally, these exercises evaluate instrument relevance and the stability of estimates under selected specifications; they do not test the exclusion restriction. First-stage strength alone therefore cannot establish the validity of settler mortality as an instrument or prove the causal interpretation.
