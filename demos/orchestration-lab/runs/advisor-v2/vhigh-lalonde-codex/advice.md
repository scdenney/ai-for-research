Keep the “favorable specifications only” conclusion, but rerun the analysis after three changes:

1. Fix the trimming comparison. The script estimates the score, trims on it, then refits a new score model inside the trimmed sample. This conflates trimming with propensity-model re-estimation. Estimate each propensity score once on the full composite, retain that score for both trimmed and untrimmed estimators, and regenerate all results. Alternatively, label the current rows “trim and refit,” but they would no longer isolate common-support trimming.

2. Add post-adjustment balance diagnostics. The memo currently says conditioning produces CPS controls with similar observed characteristics without demonstrating that it does. Add maximum absolute standardized mean difference, the number of covariates exceeding 0.10, and—especially for replacement matching—the number and effective sample size of distinct matched controls. Five-score stratification may leave substantial residual imbalance; without these diagnostics, apparent estimator sensitivity could partly be failed balance.

3. Remove “only 3 of 8 intervals cover the benchmark” as inferential evidence. Every observational estimate and the experimental benchmark share the same NSW-treated outcome mean, so their uncertainties are correlated. For a direct recovery test, form the gap and its standard error; the shared treated component cancels, leaving comparison between the adjusted CPS control mean and randomized NSW control mean. Otherwise discuss interval coverage explicitly as visual description only.

I would also soften “ATT among its 185 treated participants” to “experimental benchmark for the NSW treated sample” and call the nearest-neighbor variance a “conditional Abadie–Imbens-style approximation,” since it omits propensity-score estimation uncertainty. After these corrections, the substantive adjudication will probably remain unchanged, but it will rest on a cleaner specification comparison and defensible evidence about recovery.
