**Verdict: accept with one revision. The estimates, the figure, and the adjudication all hold up — but the submission misclassifies which Goodman-Bacon comparisons are actually contaminated, and the memo justifies its headline estimator with a property that is doing no work here. Both are factual errors in the deliverables, and both fixes fit inside the one allowed revision cycle.**

**Required fix 1 — the contaminated share is 5.4%, not 13.7%.** In `bacondecomp`'s naming, "Earlier vs Later Treated" (8.3% of weight) compares an earlier-treated cohort against a later cohort *during the later cohort's untreated periods* — the control there is not-yet-treated, which is clean under parallel trends and is exactly the comparison group the Callaway-Sant'Anna `"notyettreated"` option uses on purpose. Only "Later vs Earlier Treated" (5.4%) puts an already-treated unit in the control role — the type the brief explicitly asks you to isolate. The table's rows are separated correctly, but the table note, the memo, and the script's printed line all pool the two into "13.7% contaminated / already-treated as controls," and the script's `cat` line literally prints the 13.7% sum under the label "later-vs-earlier." Correct accounting: 94.6% clean, 5.4% forbidden — which *strengthens* the memo's own conclusion. While fixing it, add the sentence the decomposition is begging for: the forbidden cell averages +0.0046 against roughly −0.041 for everything else (attenuation in exactly the direction Goodman-Bacon predicts when effects grow), and reallocating its 5.4% weight to the clean average would move TWFE by about −0.002 — roughly two-thirds of the entire TWFE-vs-CS gap. That turns the decomposition from a weight table into a quantified bias account.

**Required fix 2 — drop "doubly robust" as the reason for the headline choice.** Double robustness in Callaway-Sant'Anna refers to the Sant'Anna–Zhao covariate adjustment (outcome regression plus propensity weighting); this specification uses no covariates, so the DR machinery is idle, and DR has nothing to do with negative-weight contamination in any case. CS is immune to contamination because each ATT(g,t) block only ever uses never- or not-yet-treated controls, by construction — say that instead. Same error in the table: Roth–Sant'Anna is the *efficient* estimator under random treatment timing, not "doubly-robust weighting," and that stronger assumption and different weighting is the honest explanation for why it sits at −0.047 while everything else sits at −0.040 — worth one clause so readers don't misread the RS gap as contamination.

**Worth folding into the same pass.** First, a composition caveat on the dynamic profile: e=+1 is identified from the 2004 and 2006 cohorts only, and e=+2 from the 2004 cohort alone (20 counties), so "effects grow: −0.02 → −0.05 → −0.14" conflates within-cohort dynamics with changing cohort composition — one clause fixes it. Second, `min_e = -2` silently discards estimable pre-period coefficients at e=−3 and e=−4; plotting all pre-periods, or citing `att_gt`'s Wald pre-test p-value from `summary()`, strengthens the parallel-trends paragraph at zero cost. Third, the figure hand-builds pointwise ±1.96·SE intervals from a procedure whose native output is simultaneous bands (`crit.val.egt`); either use the uniform critical value or label the intervals pointwise — uniform bands would actually make the borderline t=−1 coefficient look *less* alarming. Trivia: state which of `staggered`'s two SEs (`se` vs `se_neyman`) the table reports, and the memo's "0.3–1.0 percentage points" are log points.
