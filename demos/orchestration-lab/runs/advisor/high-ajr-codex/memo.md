# Memo: what the AJR base-sample stress tests support

The packaged 64-country AJR base sample reproduces the headline contrast. In the bivariate model, the OLS association between protection against expropriation and log GDP per capita is 0.522 (conventional SE 0.061). Instrumenting protection with log settler mortality gives a 2SLS estimate of 0.944 (SE reported in the robustness table). The first stage has the expected negative sign: a one-unit increase in log settler mortality predicts a 0.607-unit reduction in the protection measure, with a partial excluded-instrument F statistic of 22.95. The estimate is positive and conventionally distinguishable from zero; the F statistic clears the usual screening rule for relevance, but does not itself establish inference or validate the instrument.

The positive IV estimate remains informative under two modest changes in covariate adjustment. With absolute latitude, the 2SLS coefficient is 0.996, with its conventional SE reported in the table, and the first-stage F is 13.09. With Africa and Asia indicators, the corresponding values are 0.839 and 11.01. Each estimate is positive, conventionally distinguishable from zero, and appreciably larger than its corresponding OLS estimate (0.468 and 0.434). The continent-adjusted first stage is nevertheless close enough to 10 to warrant more caution than the bivariate model. These are limited sample-robustness checks within the same 64-country dataset; they are not evidence of external validity.

The sample perturbations are more revealing. Removing Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and the United States produces a 2SLS point estimate of 1.281, but the first-stage F falls to 8.65. By the stated rule of thumb this is weak identification, so conventional IV inference is unreliable and the larger coefficient cannot be treated as confirmation that the effect strengthens without the neo-Europes. It also does not refute the base result: weak identification makes the IV estimate uninformative for that comparison. In the Africa-only sample, the first stage essentially collapses (coefficient -0.108; F = 0.30). The resulting 2SLS point estimate is not evidence of a large African effect; with relevance absent, conventional 2SLS uncertainty measures are not trustworthy. The positive Africa-only OLS association (0.302, with conventional SE in the table) is a different, non-causal estimand and does not repair the failed instrument.

The manuscript may claim that the positive base-sample IV estimate is reproducible and remains informative with latitude or broad continent controls, conditional on the full IV identifying assumptions, especially instrument exogeneity and exclusion. First-stage strength establishes relevance only. It may report that the IV estimate exceeds OLS in the three non-weak specifications. It may not describe the relationship as established across regional or non-neo-European samples, infer a precise Africa-specific causal effect, or use the weakly identified estimates as corroboration. The failed subset first stages leave regional generalizability unresolved.
