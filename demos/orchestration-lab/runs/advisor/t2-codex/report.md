*Figure 1. Average marginal component effects (AMCEs) on the probability that a profile is selected. Points are reliability-corrected estimates from `projoint`; horizontal whiskers are respondent-clustered 95% confidence intervals. Open points denote the reference level for each attribute.*

Using `projoint`'s profile-level conjoint estimator, we obtain reliability-corrected AMCEs showing that several attributes substantially shift profile choice. Relative to the reference profile with 20% less crime than the national average, an area with 20% more crime was 25.1 percentage points less likely to be chosen (95% CI: -33.4 to -16.8), the largest absolute contrast. Longer daily driving time also had large negative effects: 75 minutes, versus 10 minutes, reduced choice probability by 23.7 points (95% CI: -31.0 to -16.5), and moving from 15% to 40% of pre-tax income in housing cost reduced it by 19.8 points (95% CI: -27.4 to -12.2). The two largest positive contrasts concerned place type. Compared with a downtown mixed-use city location, a small town increased selection by 15.8 points (95% CI: 7.6 to 24.0), and a suburban neighborhood with shops, houses, and businesses increased it by 14.5 points (95% CI: 6.8 to 22.4). Rural location (+13.5 points) and school quality of 9 rather than 5 out of 10 (+11.6 points) were next. By contrast, smaller partisan and racial-composition contrasts have confidence intervals that cross zero in estimates. Respondent-clustered 95% intervals exclude zero for these largest contrasts, whereas several smaller contrasts are imprecisely estimated.
