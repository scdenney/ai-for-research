The draft's "From then on, the movement … largely stopped" and its conclusion also conflict with both scholarly sources: Kim reports 7,378 Koreans arrested in Japanese waters in July 1946 alone, and Morris-Suzuki reports 45,960 arrests of entrants from Korea by 1951.

## Things to know

- Kim's own figure comes from the same survey Morris-Suzuki cites (Cho Rinsik: 80% came "on account of hard living"), so the two sources corroborate each other.
- Kim quotes SCAPIN 1015 in Korean, which matches the scan.
- The SCAPIN scan has no text layer. Intake has to read it as an image.
