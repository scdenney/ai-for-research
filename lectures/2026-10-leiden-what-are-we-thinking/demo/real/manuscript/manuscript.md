# Return and Re-entry: Cholera and the Korea–Japan Border, 1946

## Introduction

At the end of the Pacific War some two million Koreans lived in Japan. This paper follows
what happened to them in 1946, the year in which repatriation, cholera, and a new border
control regime met at the Korea Strait.

## Repatriation

Repatriation from Japan was largely completed in 1946. Of the more than half a million
Koreans who registered in March of that year as wishing to go home, most were shipped to
Korea by the end of the year [@morris-suzuki2011]. The quarantine station at Pusan has
been described as the most effective in occupied Asia [@lee2019].

## Cholera

Cholera reached Pusan on 1 May 1946, aboard a repatriation ship from Guangdong carrying
some 3,100 Korean returnees [@kim2021]. Conditions in Korea were hard for those who came
home. Kim writes that "about 80 percent of returnees went back to Japan because of the
hardship of life" [@kim2021, my translation].

## The border closes

On 12 June 1946, SCAP suspended the repatriation of Koreans because of the cholera
epidemic [@scapin1015]. From then on, the movement of people across the Korea Strait
largely stopped.

## Conclusion

The cholera epidemic of 1946 closed a border that repatriation had already emptied of
traffic.
