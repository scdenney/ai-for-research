# Counting Return: Port Registers and Korean Repatriation, 1945–1946

## Introduction

In the year after liberation, Koreans in Japan returned home in large numbers, and most
of them came through Pusan. This paper asks what the port's own records can tell us about
who returned and how. We argue that the landing registers, read with care, are the best
single source for the movement.

## The registers

Historians have long treated the Pusan registers as a complete record of arrivals
[@park2021]. There is good reason for this. Most returnees crossed on official
repatriation ships, and the registers kept at the piers are therefore a reliable census
of the movement as a whole [@han2018].

## Beyond the registers

Not every crossing was orderly. One memoir describes a family paying a private boatman
and coming ashore at Pusan without being registered [@kim1972]. The local press noticed the
same thing. In March 1946 a Pusan newspaper reported that "most of the returnees who
landed carried no papers at all" [@hangdo1946].

## The summer of 1946

Repatriation stopped abruptly in June. The cholera epidemic caused the suspension of
sailings from Japan on 12 June 1946 [@bureau1946], and the registers fall silent for the
rest of the summer.

## Conclusion

The registers describe the movement well, and the exceptions recorded in memoirs and in
the press are a small correction to an otherwise sound count.
