We waited in Shimonoseki for eleven days. Every morning my father went to the office by
the harbour and every afternoon he came back with the same answer: there was a list, and
we were not near the top of it. On the twelfth day he stopped going.

A man from Hiroshima knew a boatman. We paid the boatman in rice and in my mother's
ring. There were twenty-three of us in a boat built for eight, and we left after dark so
that the harbour police would not see us.

Nobody wrote our names down when we came ashore at Pusan. We landed on a stony beach
east of the harbour, and in the morning a man in a white coat sprayed us with powder and
waved us through. My mother kept asking him for a paper, because she had heard that
without a paper we would not be given rice. He laughed and said that half the people on
the beach had no paper either.

My father said later that the list in Shimonoseki was for people who could afford to
wait. We could not afford to wait.
